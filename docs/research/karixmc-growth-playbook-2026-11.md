# KarixMC organic promotion playbook

Research reviewed **November 11, 2026**. Budget: **no paid spend**. Status:
**private campaign preparation; no publication, creator contact, or mailing
authorization**. The owner stopped the recurring reviewer and requires a new
explicit instruction to start mailing. Existing contact holds and the three
worldwide introduction drafts remain unchanged. Installing a skill does not
release any of these restrictions.

The recommendation is to make the first real product experience worth showing:
an honest attempt, a visible result, and a useful explanation. More unsolicited
messages are not a substitute for that evidence. Virality, audience growth,
creator agreement, revenue, or a positive review cannot be guaranteed.

## What was actually checked

The public [KarixMC homepage](https://karixmc.pl/) returned HTTP 200 during this
review. A bounded HTML read showed a Minecraft directory, account linking,
play-reward and in-game-store claims, and three clearly labeled KarixMC-operated
demo worlds. Displayed online counts were zero at that observation; the page
showed eleven profiles. Those counters are a momentary website observation,
not independently verified users or demand. The page describes points and
items rather than player cash payouts. Its initial HTML contained a title and
description, but no Open Graph/Twitter metadata or canonical link was found by
the bounded metadata check. This does not establish how every rendered route
behaves. No login, Minecraft join, account link, reward calculation, store
delivery, payment, mobile usability, or analytics event was exercised.

The two saved English/Polish briefs already require an independent test before
recommendation. Compensation fields remain individually negotiated, with no
saved percentage or earning window. Organic preparation must not rewrite those
commercial campaigns, request free labor from creators, promise a giveaway, or
quietly turn a contingent commission into a new spending commitment.

## A material question before public promotion

The current [Minecraft Usage Guidelines](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/usage-guidelines)
restrict cross-server use/transfer of in-game virtual currency. KarixMC's public
portable-balance positioning creates an unresolved policy-fit question. The
actual point, redemption and server architecture must be compared with the
guidelines before promoting that benefit. This review cannot decide the legal
classification or establish compliance; do not disguise it by renaming currency.
Record the owner's resolution and supporting evidence, or retain the release
block. The guidelines also address non-affiliation disclosures, influencer
relationships, suitable content and gameplay advantages. Reward entitlements
need the same review. Original gameplay commentary and appropriately placed
disclosures matter when preparing video material.

This is a release prerequisite, not permission to alter the product, claim
Mojang approval, or silently change the saved offer. Preparing directory and
onboarding storyboards can continue, with any unresolved reward claim marked
as blocked. Do not publish a cut that merely hides a relevant product issue.

## Campaigns to prepare first

These are editorial proposals, not proof that the depicted result works.
Use original owner-recorded footage or material with explicit reuse permission.
Never synthesize a busy server, a transaction, a creator endorsement, a receipt,
or a participant reaction. Draft both English and Polish captions; have a fluent
reader check the intended language before release.

| Priority / concept | Opening and visual device | Proof required | One next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. **The uncut first attempt** | A new tester starts from the directory. A visible attempt counter replaces a cinematic promise. | A fresh authorized test, real timestamps, clear demo ownership, complete outcome including failure. Reward segments require the policy resolution above. | Read the current joining instructions and decide whether to try. |
| 2. **Zero players, honest build diary** | The founder shows the empty demo and explains one friction point fixed this week. | Real dated screen/game captures and an actual before/after. No invented community or review. | Give feedback on that one onboarding step through an approved public feedback route. |
| 3. **Which world fits your evening?** | Three short original scenes compare the listed play styles, with the same simple visual treatment. | The same tested client/version, dated directory information and authentic gameplay; no claim that a listing guarantees quality. | Choose a listed world based on a stated preference. |
| 4. **One bug, one visible fix** | Show a specific confusing screen, the change, and a clean retest. | A reproduced problem, authorized fix, successful retest and redacted identifiers. | Read the short setup guide. |
| 5. **The independent test card** | A single page gives testers the exact joining task and space to report what failed. | Accurate current steps, conditions and known limits; no required positive verdict. | Save the checklist or voluntarily submit feedback. |
| 6. **Server-owner field notes** | Explain one real integration question with a small diagram and a reproducible example. | Verified plugin/setup facts and the unresolved-policy status. No secrets, speculative player income or untested compatibility. | Review the integration documentation; no sales pressure. |

The first two concepts should be the initial pair. They make a small, early
network credible without borrowing a larger community's appearance. Concepts
three through six answer actual questions and can become searchable references.
There is no need to pad a daily content quota with repetitive variations.

### Ready-to-produce outline: the uncut first attempt

Prepare a 25–40 second edit and retain the original recording for review. The
timings are a proposed edit, not a claim about how quickly onboarding completes.

| Edit | Material to record | Draft caption direction |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 seconds | Tester at a genuine starting screen. | EN: “First attempt. Let's see where I get stuck.” PL: “Pierwsza próba. Sprawdźmy, gdzie utknę.” |
| 3–10 seconds | The actual world selection and the demo label. | State who operates the demo and why that world was selected. |
| 10–25 seconds | One meaningful joining step, shown clearly. | Explain the action, not an unsupported benefit. Keep mistakes visible or label edits. |
| 25–35 seconds | The real outcome: success, delay, or failure. | Describe only the observed result and the tested version/date. |
| Final frame | A readable next step and product identity. | One relevant instruction; no timer, fake scarcity, cash claim, or invented endorsement. |

Record the opening two ways: **curiosity** (“Where do I get stuck?”) and
**clarity** (“Here is the joining process”). Keep the actual proof segment the
same. These are two creative hypotheses, not an instruction to upload duplicate
clips or an A/B result. Use a native concurrent test only where the account and
format support it; otherwise report comparisons as observational.

### Ready-to-produce outline: the honest build diary

Prepare one founder narration answering: what a first-time player sees, which
step was confusing, what changed, what remains uncertain, and what feedback
would help. Show one concrete improvement rather than claiming a revolution.
Finish with the single feedback question. A recurring series is appropriate
only when there is a new substantive change; silence is better than fabricated
milestones. Keep bank details, authentication screens, player identifiers,
private replies, usernames without permission and administrative tools out of
the recording.

## Distribution choices and their boundaries

| Channel | Organic material that fits | Evidence or permission before release |
|---|---|---|
| Owner-controlled video channel | Original narrated test, useful guide, honest development diary. | Owner confirms the account, exact artifact, caption, destination and publication permission. No new account or connection is assumed. |
| Owner-controlled website | A clear first-session guide, known-limit FAQ, real demo labels and accurate preview card. | Website owner reviews changes and the policy question. A website change belongs to its own authorized repository/deployment flow. |
| Existing opt-in community | One substantive update or an announced test session with practical instructions. | Current community rules and an appropriate channel; participants choose to join. No unsolicited DMs, mass invites or friend-request campaign. |
| Relevant moderated communities | A self-contained technical explanation or project feedback request, adapted for that community. | Read current rules and obtain moderator approval where required. Disclose ownership. Do not contact moderators until separately authorized. |
| Creators | A future independent-test conversation with a genuinely suitable creator. | Explicit mailing resumption, current source/contact qualification, fresh complete mailbox sync, all existing limits and exact-copy approval. Held contacts stay held. |
| Search | A small set of tested, useful setup and troubleshooting pages. | First-hand evidence, accurate titles, author/date and maintained instructions; no mass-generated location/server/keyword pages. |

YouTube's [spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)
rules out repetitive high-volume comments, artificial engagement and flooding
with near-identical automated content. Do not exchange rewards for views,
likes or subscriptions. Its [branded-content guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235?hl=en)
requires relationship disclosure; calling an asset “organic” does not erase a
commercial relationship.

[Discord](https://discord.com/guidelines) prohibits unsolicited bulk messages
and user-bots. [Reddit's spam policy](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043504051-Spam)
also covers repeated or unsolicited mass engagement, including manual posting;
community moderators set additional rules. These are reasons to prepare useful
native material and permission notes, not tactics for rotating accounts,
evading filters or disguising ownership.

For search, follow the [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
and [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies):
one genuinely useful tested guide is preferable to scaled pages made primarily
to manipulate rankings. No promise of indexing or rankings follows from a draft.

## Email is a separate, held channel

Do not upload a purchased list, scrape private/gated contacts, guess addresses,
send a bulk blast, add accounts, or increase the existing pacing limits. A
published business address is evidence to review, not blanket campaign consent.
Future creator proposals retain canonical ownership, recent Minecraft content,
same-channel business wording, individual contact authorization, deduplication,
suppression, source-grounded copy, real expiry checks and exact approval.

Google's [sender guidance](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en)
distinguishes ordinary and higher-volume requirements, recommends wanted mail
and steady sending, and warns that spam complaints and bursts damage delivery.
Its technical thresholds are not a target, a right to send, or authorization to
raise Hermes's limits. This review neither audits the sender's authentication
nor establishes permission under any recipient's local law. Any future opt-in
newsletter needs its own explicit scope, consent history and unsubscribe design;
it is not implemented by this playbook.

## Measure activation before reach

Use a dated experiment sheet or an approved first-party export. No analytics
account, pixel, tracking SDK or new integration has been installed by this
research. If an event is unavailable, mark it **not measured**. Do not substitute
registered profiles, SMTP acceptances, or video starts for successful product use.

| Metric | Definition to agree before release |
|---|---|
| Qualified visit | A non-test landing session from the exact approved asset/source; record exclusions. |
| Joining start | A visitor intentionally opens the joining instructions or starts the defined joining flow. |
| First successful session | A unique eligible tester joins a specified tested world and completes a defined meaningful play action. Use an actual source event, not a page view. |
| Verified reward / delivery | Separate observed product events, only if policy review permits the feature and an authoritative event exists. |
| Time to first successful session | Elapsed time from the agreed start event; record abandonment and failures separately. |
| Day-seven return | A participant from the original activated cohort returns within the agreed window. Count only cohorts old enough to have completed that window. |
| Voluntary sharing | A genuine share/referral event if measurable; exclude paid, forced, rewarded engagement and owner testing. |
| Friction / trust guardrails | Broken joins, unsupported claims, privacy complaints, moderator removals, bounces or opt-outs as applicable. |

UTM tags can distinguish approved sources when the website's analytics actually
captures them. Google's [campaign URL documentation](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952?hl=en)
explains the source/medium/campaign fields. Use lowercase stable values and no
emails, player names, private IDs or other personal data in URLs. For example,
an eventual approved asset could use `utm_campaign=first_session_01` and
`utm_content=clarity_en`; a tagged link alone does not install attribution or
prove an activation.

For video, review retained viewing and the quality of comments alongside
traffic, using the platform's [content analytics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en).
Do not call the edit with the most starts a winner. YouTube's
[native title/thumbnail testing](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)
has format/account restrictions and can return no clear winner; it is not a
Shorts testing capability. Independent posts published at different times do
not provide a randomized test.

## The first experiment, after a separate release

**Hypothesis:** a clear, honest first-session explanation helps visitors complete
onboarding more reliably than a curiosity-led opening.

1. Define the actual successful-session event and verify that it can be measured
   before recruiting anyone. Use internal tests without public promotion first.
2. Prepare the two openings above, with the same evidence, language, destination
   and next step. Record what differs and what is held constant.
3. Have a small consenting usability group try the flow after owner release.
   Observe failure reasons; do not present a convenience sample as the market.
4. If traffic supports a valid concurrent test, predefine allocation, duration,
   minimum worthwhile improvement, sample requirement and stopping rules. At
   very low traffic, report raw counts and uncertainty instead of manufacturing
   significance or selecting a winner after every new visit.
5. Stop release on broken onboarding, an unresolved product claim or a privacy/
   platform concern. Reproduce and fix the issue before another distribution run.
6. Review activated visitors and mature return cohorts. Change one meaningful
   variable for the next experiment; do not change the product, offer and channel
   simultaneously and attribute the result to a better hook.

There is no measured baseline for this experiment yet. Preparation has produced
no new visitors, posts, creator contacts, collaborations, revenue or viral lift.

## Preparation sequence, with no automatic launch date

| Stage | Reviewable output | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| A. Evidence | Dated claim ledger, exact tested path, known gaps, policy-fit decision. | Unsupported claims remain blocked. |
| B. Core material | Original recording plan, two short openings, one first-session guide, EN/PL caption drafts. | Rights, privacy, accuracy and language review. |
| C. Channel fit | One suitable owned channel and at most one relevant community proposal, with current rules and an explicit next step. | No account assumed, no moderator/creator contact sent. |
| D. Measurement | Event definitions, baseline availability, cohort windows and one experiment card. | Unknown events cannot be reported as outcomes. |
| E. Release review | Exact artifact, destination, account, time, disclosures, holds and owner decision. | Still blocked until separately authorized; mailing also requires the explicit start-mailing instruction. |

Hermes's proposed promotion planning skills map to these stages:
`promotion-brief`, `organic-campaigns`, `community-fit`, `creator-fit`,
`content-repurpose`, `landing-review`, `experiment-design`, and
`promotion-release-review`. Their source installation, native availability and
runtime validation are separate evidence; this document does not assert that
all eight have been loaded or executed. None supplies outbound authority.

The next useful result is a tested, truthful first-session proof and a small
set of reviewable organic assets. External release, additional creator emails,
new commercial commitments and website deployments remain separate decisions.
